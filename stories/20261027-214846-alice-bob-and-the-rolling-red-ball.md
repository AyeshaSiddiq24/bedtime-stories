Alice, Bob, and the Rolling Red Ball

Once upon a time, there was a girl named Alice. Her best friend Bob was a pirate cat. He was gray all over, except for one white paw. He wore a tiny black hat with a feather. He loved fish more than anything in the whole wide sea. "Ahoy!" he would say.

One afternoon, the two friends went to the park. Bob's whiskers twitched. "I smell fish!" he cried. "Pirates always hunt for treasure."

Alice threw her red ball high. It bounced under a wooden bench and rolled into a tangle of bushes. Before Alice could reach it, Bob gave it a quick nudge with his paw. Tap! It rolled even deeper.

"Bob!" Alice gasped. "Did you do that on purpose?"

Bob only blinked his big green eyes. He tipped his hat and grinned like a pirate with a secret.

"Oh, bother," Alice grumbled. She marched off toward the swings without him. Her cheeks felt hot.

A boy on the swing laughed. "Pirate cats? That's silly. Cats just nap all day."

Alice turned around. "A best friend can be anyone," she said, "even a cat with a pirate hat!"

Then she walked slowly back to the bench. Bob was sitting there quietly. He had waited the whole time. His ears were flat and sad. His hat sat crooked.

"I'm sorry I pushed your ball," he said softly. "I thought it was a treasure trail."

Alice knelt in the grass. "Show me again," she said. "Best friends listen, and best friends help."

Bob pressed his ear to the wooden fence. He held very still. Alice heard it too, a faint tap, tap, rattle. The ball was rolling on the other side.

Bob sniffed along the bottom of the fence. He found a tiny gap, no bigger than a loaf of bread.

"Ahoy! Found it!" Bob meowed.

"Best friends listen, and best friends help," Alice whispered.

Bob slipped through the gap like a shadow. Rustle, rustle. Then the red ball popped out from under the fence. Bob pushed it back to her with his nose.

Alice hugged him so tight that he purred. "Thank you for showing me," she said.

"Arr, no need," said Bob with a happy wiggle. "Pirates forgive quickly. Especially when there's fish for supper."

They rolled the ball all the way home. Bob chased it across the grass, his hat bouncing.

On the garden step, Bob ate his silver fish one bite at a time. "The finest treasure on the whole seven seas," he sighed. The sky turned soft and purple. One by one, the first stars blinked awake.

"One," Alice counted. "Two. Three."

She yawned a big, slow yawn. She leaned against her friend's warm, gray fur. The crickets hummed. Bob's purr grew slower and softer, like a little drum.

The last star winked. Alice's eyes closed. Bob tucked his white paw under his chin, and he closed his eyes too.
